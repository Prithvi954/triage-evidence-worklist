## Red-flag rules (practice only)

A rule fires only when the note says it about the patient, now. It does not fire if the patient denies it, if it's about a family member, or if it's in the past.

| Rule | Fires when the note says | Why | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chest pain with warning signs | chest pain + one of: sweating, sweaty, diaphoretic, shortness of breath, short of breath, nausea, left arm, jaw, spreading, radiating | Chest pain with sweating, breathlessness, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw can mean a heart attack. | ☐ |
| Possible stroke signs | facial droop, face droop, face is drooping, arm weakness, weakness on one side, slurred speech, trouble speaking, can't find words | Sudden face droop, arm weakness, or speech trouble can mean a stroke. Note the time the signs started. | ☐ |
| Severe trouble breathing | trouble breathing, difficulty breathing, struggling to breathe, can't breathe, unable to speak in full sentences, lips look blue, blue lips | Struggling to breathe or being unable to speak in full sentences needs immediate attention. | ☐ |
| Fever with confusion or low blood pressure (possible sepsis) | fever + one of: confused, confusion, low blood pressure, blood pressure low, very fast heart rate | Infection with confusion or low blood pressure can mean sepsis, which gets worse quickly. | ☐ |
| Severe bleeding | severe bleeding, bleeding heavily, uncontrolled bleeding, bleeding through | Bleeding that won't stop can quickly become life-threatening. | ☐ |
| Possible severe allergic reaction | throat tightness, throat feels tight, tongue swelling, swollen tongue, lips swelling | Throat tightness or tongue swelling after an exposure can mean anaphylaxis. | ☐ |
| Unresponsive | unresponsive, not responding, hard to wake | A patient who is hard to wake or not responding needs immediate assessment. | ☐ |

Acuity uses ESI 1–5 (1 = immediate, 5 = non-urgent). "ESI 2 or more urgent" means 1 or 2 counts as correct.
