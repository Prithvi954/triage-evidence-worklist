## A. Anthropic product, data handling, and policy

**Runtime route chosen for the prototype:** the direct commercial **Claude Messages API** (`POST https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages`), called from our backend with the official Python SDK, model `claude-opus-5-5`. We do not use claude.ai chat, the Console/Workbench, Amazon Bedrock, Google Vertex, Microsoft Foundry, or any gateway. The hackathon sends **synthetic fixtures only**.

### Conflicts and unresolved points (recorded, not resolved in our favor)

1. **Default retention: "30 days" vs "not retained by default."** The privacy center (updated 2026-07-01) says API inputs and outputs are deleted *within 30 days*. The API docs page (checked 2026-11-13) says conversation content "is not retained by default; the exception is Covered Models." Both are about the first-party API. **Design assumption: up to 30 days of retention, plus flagged-content exceptions.** Resolve against the executed agreement.
2. **How to get a BAA for the API.** The API docs say eligible organizations "can review and execute the BAA ... directly from the Claude Console." The support article (relative date "over 2 weeks ago") says the Primary Owner signs and then contacts Anthropic or Sales to enable it, and describes no Console self-serve path. **Unresolved. Treat as a procurement step.**
3. **Whether the Usage Policy's high-risk rules cover clinician-facing (B2B) tools.** An older Anthropic post scoped high-risk requirements to consumer-facing outputs. The **current Usage Policy (effective 2026-11-12)** defines "High-risk AI Recommendations" by area, including "**Healthcare access: setting triage or waitlist priority**," with no B2B carve-out. Its exclusions include "internal drafting, research, summarization, or analysis that is not the ultimate High-risk AI Recommendation delivered." Our extraction is arguably excluded, but the evidence inbox influences who gets reviewed. **We comply with the requirements anyway** and flag this for counsel.

### Anthropic Usage Policy (vendor policy, not law)

[Usage Policy PDF, effective November 12, 2026](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/6ae2f5bb8675bcd9fac006125817ba1393b396e4.pdf); [2026 update announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update).

- **Qualified human in the loop:** "A qualified person must meaningfully review a High-risk AI Recommendation, with the authority to change or modify it, before it is delivered ... or before it is used to implement a decision." The reviewer must hold a license where law requires one and "remains responsible."
- **Disclosure:** an individual "who is the subject of a decision based on a High-risk AI Recommendation must be clearly told that AI was used."
- **Covered area:** Medical (interpreting symptoms "to triage") and Healthcare access ("setting triage or waitlist priority").
- **Vendor policy versus federal law:** These are contractual conditions for using Anthropic's products. They "do not replace local legal requirements." HIPAA, FDA device law, and state law apply on their own terms, whether or not Anthropic's policy does.
- **Design decisions:** Only an authorized clinician action changes priority. Every AI item is labeled "AI-assisted, unverified." The clinician can correct or reject each field. **Patient-facing disclosure of AI involvement is a facility decision** (see approvals in `05-operating-model.md`).

**Emergency flags (added later).** The rule-based "possible emergency" flags are closer to FDA example 11 (software that analyzes patient information to detect a possibly life-threatening condition and alerts a clinician) than the evidence extraction is. They are probably a device function, which makes a regulatory assessment *more* urgent. Mitigations in the design: the rules and the exact triggering sentence are always shown, a nurse must check and write a reason, and only the charge nurse's decision changes priority. None of that makes the alert non-device by itself.

**FDA, applied to this exact workflow (a reasoned analysis, not a regulatory determination).** The app analyzes patient-specific free text and flags a patient's note for review in an urgent-care setting. In our favor: it shows a *list* of evidence with exact quotes, it does not output a priority or diagnosis, it does not alarm, and the clinician can read the original note before acting (criteria 3 and 4). Against us: the setting is time-sensitive, so a busy nurse may rely on the label (automation bias, which FDA now names under criterion 4), and the inbox status label may steer attention. **Requires a qualified regulatory assessment:** whether the inbox label or the announcement is an "alert" supporting time-critical decisions; whether the intended-use statement and labeling are sufficient; whether a future version that orders reviews by AI output would change the analysis.

## C. Clinical evidence: three systems

| | **NegEx** (extraction from free text) | **NEWS2** (established deterioration-review approach) | **Kaiser Advance Alert Monitor (AAM)** (deployed alert and escalation workflow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source | Chapman et al., *J Biomed Inform* 2001;34:301–310. [PDF](https://www.dbmi.pitt.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/A-simple-algorithm-for-identifying-negated-findings-and-diseases-in-discharge-summaries.pdf) | Royal College of Physicians, Dec 2017; 2020 additional guidance. [Thresholds chart](https://www.rcp.ac.uk/media/2acdezkd/news2-chart-2_news-thresholds-and-triggers_0.pdf); [2020 guidance](https://www.rcp.ac.uk/media/umzn4ntq/news2_additional-guidance-002-_0.pdf) | Escobar et al., *NEJM* 2020;383:1951–60. [PMC7787261](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7787261) |
| Population / setting | Sentences from discharge summaries (1,000 sentences, 1,235 findings). | Adult acute-care patients (UK). | Adult inpatients, 21 Kaiser Northern California hospitals, staggered rollout 2016–2019. |
| Inputs | Indexed findings plus regex negation phrases. | Six physiological parameters plus supplemental oxygen use. | Hourly EHR data (vitals, labs, and more) into a predictive score. |
| Intended user | Researchers and NLP pipelines. | Bedside nurses and ward teams. | A **remote team of specially trained nurses** reviews alerts first, then contacts the rapid response team. |
| Action | Label a finding negated or not. | Score aggregate; ≥5 triggers urgent review (2020 guidance: assessment within 60 minutes if new); ≥7 triggers critical-care-competent assessment. | Remote nurse review, then escalation to bedside teams. Results were not shown directly to floor staff, to limit alert fatigue. |
| Outcome measured | Specificity 94.5%, PPV 84.5% (vs. baseline 85.3% / 68.4%); lower sensitivity. | Escalation thresholds (guideline, not a trial). | Observational comparison: lower 30-day mortality after alert (reported 15.8% vs 20.4%), fewer ICU admissions. |
| Limitations | **Extraction accuracy only, on historical notes.** No patient outcome. Does not model subject or time. | Physiology only; misses deterioration that is documented only in prose; requires local threshold changes. | **Not randomized.** The effect belongs to the whole workflow (staffing plus process), not the model alone. |

**Cautionary deployed example:** an external validation of the Epic Sepsis Model (Wong et al., *JAMA Intern Med* 2021; Michigan Medicine, 38,455 hospitalizations) found hospitalization-level AUC **0.63**. It missed 67% of sepsis cases while alerting on 18% of all hospitalizations. Epic disputed the method. ([coverage](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/research-suggests-epic-sepsis-model-lacking-predictive-power); primary article in JAMA Internal Medicine.) An extraction study is not evidence about care outcomes: **a model correctly labeling historical notes is not a clinical team reaching the right patient sooner.** AAM is the only one of these that measured patient outcomes, and it measured a whole staffed workflow.

**LLM extraction:** Agrawal et al., EMNLP 2022 ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.130)): GPT-3-era models were competitive few-shot clinical information extractors on re-annotated datasets, including medication status. That is extraction accuracy only, on a different model, so it is not transferable evidence for Claude.

## D. Research finding → design decision

| Finding | Decision in this prototype |
|---|---|
| NegEx-style rules already handle negation well (our baseline: assertion 9/9) | Claude's job is the harder part: subject, time context, and comparison across notes. The demo shows this honestly, along with the case where a quote drops "denies." |
| Exact quotes can still be misread (FDA criterion 4; NIST confabulation risk) | The validator checks quotes against the stored note, shows the **full sentence**, and runs deterministic cue checks. Clinician corrections are recorded. |
| AAM worked through a staffed review team, not a raw alert | Evidence goes to an inbox that a nurse owns and that has deadlines. The charge nurse is the backup. No direct-to-floor alarm. |
| Epic sepsis: alert burden and missed cases | Nothing is suppressed by AI. Every note gets a review item. No AI-driven ordering. The unreviewed count is always visible. |
| FDA: alarms for time-critical conditions are device functions | Generic "awaiting clinical review" message. No urgency words. No priority output from the model. |
| Structured-output schemas are cached outside PHI protections | The schema contains no patient data (`app/prompt.py`). |
| Several features are not ZDR/HIPAA-eligible | Feature allowlist enforced before any request is built. |
| WCAG 4.1.3 | Polite status region; generic announcements; focus never moved by data. |
| SAFER Contingency Planning | AI-off path is complete. Feed stall is detected by heartbeat silence. |

## E. What Claude adds over a phrase matcher (same synthetic notes)

Run: `.venv/bin/python -m scripts.evaluate --mode replay` (baselines are real; the Claude row in replay mode is an **authored fixture**, not a measurement). Baseline results, 9 expected findings:

| approach | subject | assertion | time context | change vs earlier note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| phrase match | 8/9 | 6/9 | 7/9 | 3/9 (cannot compare) |
| NegEx-lite | 8/9 | **9/9** | 7/9 | 3/9 (cannot compare) |
| Claude | **not measured: no API credentials in the build environment.** Run `--mode live`. | | | |

**A case where context matters:** "Patient's mother experienced demo signal A last year." Both baselines label it a *current patient* finding. The intended Claude output is `subject=family_member`, `time=past`, and the validator warns if Claude says otherwise. **A difficulty case:** the `note_A3` replay simulates a model that quotes "demo signal A at rest" without "denies." The quote is verbatim, so it *passes* quote verification. Only the full-sentence cue check and the clinician catch it. That residual semantic error is recorded in the clinician's correction.

## F. Evidence a facility needs before real patient information enters this configuration

**Available from published vendor statements** (above): training default, standard retention, ZDR scope, feature eligibility table, Covered Model rules, Usage Policy.

**Available only through an executed agreement, organization settings, or procurement review:**
- The executed BAA version and its Eligible Services list, naming the **organization ID**, **model** (`claude-opus-5-5`), and **features** in use.
- Organization settings showing HIPAA readiness is enabled (and ZDR status, if relevant). Evidence that the API key belongs to that organization and workspace.
- The retention arrangement that applies (including the 30-day vs "not retained" conflict above) and the flagged-content exception.
- Human-access conditions and logging, the subprocessor list, processing geography (`inference_geo` if used), incident notification terms and contact, and breach timelines shorter than 60 days if negotiated.
- Security attestations (e.g., SOC 2 report) from the Trust Center under NDA.
- Internal: a HIPAA risk analysis that covers this data flow, sign-offs from the privacy officer and security reviewer, FDA regulatory assessment, accessibility testing with intended users, and clinical validation (`08-validation-plan.md`).
